# Weekly editorial guidelines

## Build the thesis before the page

Read all daily summaries and analyses, then cluster them into recurring causes, transitions, risks, and distribution patterns. Choose a lead thesis only after at least two independent stories support it.

Use this hierarchy:

1. Week title: the most important transition or tension.
2. Executive summary: three evidence-backed movements.
3. Signals: three conclusions that remain useful beyond one headline.
4. Section decks: how each category contributes to the weekly thesis.
5. Story cards: specific evidence and implications.

## Select 4 stories per category

Balance each section across:

- One or two high-impact stories with strong reach or clear consequence.
- One structural story about tools, platforms, business models, policy, or production systems.
- One distinctive story that adds a new mechanism, audience, genre, or counter-signal.
- Reasonable diversity across dates, authors, companies, and viewpoints.

Do not select four variations of the same announcement. Do not use engagement alone as the editorial decision. Use heat rankings to show reach and story cards to show breadth.

## Write implication-led copy

- Title: describe what changed or what the event proves.
- Summary sentence 1: state the verified fact.
- Summary sentence 2: state the bounded implication for AI, games, platforms, creators, or players.
- Deck: make one category-level claim, not a list of four titles.
- Signal: synthesize at least two stories; avoid predictions disguised as facts.
- Watch item: name a future observable and the evidence that would confirm or weaken it.

Useful length targets:

| Element | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Week title | 18–36 Chinese characters |
| Executive paragraph | 80–150 Chinese characters |
| Signal title | 8–18 Chinese characters |
| Signal body | 45–90 Chinese characters |
| Section deck | 45–90 Chinese characters |
| Story title | 12–30 Chinese characters |
| Story summary | 45–110 Chinese characters |
| Watch title | 12–30 Chinese characters |
| Watch body | 35–80 Chinese characters |

Treat these as editorial targets, not truncation rules.
Length targets are not hard character cuts. If copy is too long, rewrite it or remove a complete sentence or section. Never publish a partial clause, orphan heading, mid-word cut, or ellipsis created only to fit a character limit.

## Localize gameplay terminology

- Prefer established wording used by Chinese players, developers, and games media over literal translations.
- Translate the mechanic in context, not the individual English words. For example, render `extraction shooter` or `extraction RPG` as `搜打撤射击`, `搜打撤 RPG`, or `搜打撤玩法` according to the sentence—never `提取射击` or `提取 RPG`.
- When a term has no stable Chinese equivalent, keep the original English on first use and explain the mechanic briefly instead of inventing a machine-translated label.
- Before publishing, search titles, tags, summaries, recommendations, enrichment copy, daily summaries, and weekly editorial fields to keep gameplay terminology consistent.

## Handle metrics honestly

- Copy raw `views` and `likes` from daily JSON and format them for display.
- Use `万` or `亿` consistently; do not imply precision the source does not contain.
- Explain non-engagement metrics explicitly, such as wishlists, concurrent players, or release dates.
- Do not add metrics found only in an unverified secondary summary.
- Keep rankings generally ordered by views. Use log-scaled `heat` values for readable bars, with the leader at 100.

## Keep analysis calibrated

Separate these levels:

- Fact: directly present in the daily item or source tweet.
- Interpretation: a reasonable consequence supported by the fact.
- Watch: an unresolved outcome to verify next week.

Avoid certainty words when evidence is partial. Avoid turning one viral post into an industry-wide conclusion. Include counter-signals when the week contains them.

## Avoid stale watch items

Every watch item must occur after the report end date or remain unresolved at that date. Check release dates and scheduled events against the covered range. Replace items that already happened with a measurable follow-up such as retention, player response, policy change, adoption, or production evidence.
